I am proud to say that Asma Jahangir and I became close friends through our association in South Asians for Human Rights (sAHR) which we had helped to form in the early nineties and in sharing our experiences of working with Asia Pacific Women Law and Development. Through the first network we met in different parts of South Asia, as we studied the situation of minorities – religious and ethnic – in six countries in South Asia. As Co-Chair of the network Asma inspired us to investigate and document custodial torture by the states in the region. Through APWLD Asma reached out to a wider network where she could raise issues of legal reforms for women and gender based violence.

We admired Asma for her determined activism, for her inspiring leadership and her courage whether she was challenging military rule in Pakistan, speaking out against authoritarian rule in South Asia or even raising human rights concerns as an UN Rapporteru in Iran. In taking up legal causes in court or speaking out in public she was in the frontline of promoting human rights.

We will miss her courageous leadership, but we will continue to respect her legacy of facing legal challenges and defying authority. Asma’s memory will continue to inspire us even as we hope to continue to carry the torch that she has lit.
