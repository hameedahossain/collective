  • ❦ 2026 · Syed Humayun Kabir Memorial Research Symposium Remarks at the SAJIDA Foundation Conference Dr. Hossain draws three lessons from founding and running ASK with Salma Sobhan: rights matter, institutions grow by adapting to people's realities, and law shapes how people claim justice. She illustrates them with ASK's outreach, paralegals, fact-finding, shelter, counselling, the 'Jokhon Tokhon' drop-in school for working children, and its films and publications. She concludes that a good institution must keep learning. Speeches, Talks & Lectures
  • ❦ 2022 · Written for the honouring of Rounaq Jahan by… A Tribute to My Friend Rounaq Jahan Recalls Dr. Hossain's nearly fifty-year friendship with political scientist Rounaq Jahan, from their meeting in 1970 at Professor Razzaq's gatherings. It covers Jahan's founding role in Women for Women (1973), her participation in the 1975 Mexico UN Women's Conference, and her work in regional feminist networks. It ends by endorsing Bonik Barta's award to her. Tributes & Remembrances
  • ❦ 2021 · The Daily Star, 50 Years of Bangladesh… The Challenge of Change Argues that Bangladesh's improving statistics on women's advancement hide continuing inequality. Women still face early marriage, dowry, violence, poor wages and unequal personal laws, and different classes, religions and ethnic groups of women are affected differently. It calls for legal reform, real representation and a citizenship-based feminist politics that goes beyond piecemeal concessions. Gender & Development
  • ❦ 2021 · BDI Lifetime Achievement Award lecture The Challenge of Citizenship in Building a Just Society (BDI Lifetime Achievement Award Lecture) A long autobiographical lecture on how citizens have pushed for justice. It moves from refugee relief in Sindh in 1947 and work with war-affected women in 1972, through 1960s Dhaka, New Values and Forum, to the founding of Arunima and KARIKA. It goes on to the women's movement, international feminist networks, and ASK's legal aid and public-interest litigation. It ends with a call for collective action across all groups to defend democracy and rights. Speeches, Talks & Lectures
  • ❦ 2018 · Prepared for the Genocide Studies Centre A Framework of Rights for Gender Equality Looks at how international rights frameworks (CEDAW, the UPR, the UN Special Rapporteur on Violence against Women) have been used to measure and push Bangladesh's progress on gender equality. It notes that constitutional equality stops at the public sphere, that the government keeps its reservations to CEDAW Articles 2 and 16(1)(c), and that discrimination continues in personal law, work and politics. It ends by calling for sustained monitoring and advocacy by women's movements. Gender & Development
  • ❦ 2018 · Written for The Hunger Project Crossing the Boundaries Describes how girls in Bangladesh have entered sport, which used to be a male domain, and mentions the national women's football team and the 2010 South Asian Games. The main example is Suborno Char in Noakhali, where Nijera Kori and the young feminist group One Billion Rising helped village girls aged 9 to 15 form a football team despite opposition from their conservative community. Gender & Development
  • ❦ 2018 · Prothom Alo English, Opinion Remembering Asma A tribute to Pakistani human rights lawyer Asma Jahangir after her sudden death at 66. It recalls her defiance of military rulers, her UN mandates and her leadership of the HRCP. It also covers her role as first co-chair of South Asians for Human Rights, which Dr. Hossain worked with her to found, and her women's rights work. It ends by calling for collective struggle without fear or favour. Tributes & Remembrances
  • ❦ 2018 · Bangla edition of “Crossing the Boundaries” সীমানা পেরিয়ে About girls breaking into football in Bangladesh, from the national women's team to Suborno Char, a conservative village in Noakhali. There, Nijera Kori and young feminists of One Billion Rising helped girls aged 9-15 overcome community opposition, train with a professional coach and play a championship match. Dr. Hossain presents it as an example of girls resisting discrimination for themselves and for others. Gender & Developmentবাংলা
  • ❦ 2016 · South Asian Women's Fund South Asian Women's Fund: Tenth Anniversary Presentation Marks SAWF's ten years of funding grassroots women's organisations and places it within South Asian women's movements, from Roqaiya Sakhawat onwards. It reviews gains (domestic violence laws, sexual harassment guidelines, recognition of wartime rape) and the continuing obstacles of patriarchy, identity politics and conflict. It closes with a commitment to democracy, non-violence and human rights. Speeches, Talks & Lectures
  • ❦ 2016 · The Daily Star, Op-Ed Women's March for Justice (International Women's Day) Written for International Women's Day 2016 (theme 'Planet 50-50'). She acknowledges Bangladesh's MDG gains but asks whether they have freed women from forced marriage, dowry, domestic and sexual violence. She calls for reform of religion-based personal laws, criticises women MPs' silence on campus harassment, and urges a movement that takes account of class, religion, ethnicity and caste. Gender & Development
  • ❦ 2014 · Ain o Salish Kendra website It's Time to Pay Up Written ten months after the Rana Plaza collapse, this column demands fair and prompt compensation for workers killed or injured at Rana Plaza and Tazreen Fashions. It sets out casualty figures, the inadequate interim payments, the High Court-directed compensation committee and the gap between its rates and union demands based on ILO 121. It calls on brands, BGMEA, factory owners and the government to fund the Rana Plaza Trust Fund. Labour & Workers' Rights
  • ❦ 2014 · The Daily Star, 23rd Anniversary supplement Towards Tolerance and Justice Written for the Daily Star's annual review after the 2014 election, which major parties boycotted. She laments that the democratic, secular and inclusive goals of 1971 have been lost to power struggles, impunity and intolerance. She describes the attacks on Hindu communities and on women in the Chittagong Hill Tracts, and calls for challenging impunity and renewing humanist values. Human Rights, Justice & Religion
  • ❦ 2014 · Prothom Alo, মতামত তৈরি পোশাকশিল্প: শ্রমিকদের ক্ষতিপূরণ কবে দেওয়া হবে? Ten months after the Rana Plaza collapse and fifteen months after the Tazreen fire, Dr. Hossain sets out the death and injury toll and the plight of the injured and of orphaned children. She criticises delays and inadequate amounts in compensation, including the government committee's figure, which was set without trade-union input. She demands that brands, BGMEA, factory owners such as Tuba Group, and the government pay full compensation through the Rana Plaza Trust Fund. Labour & Workers' Rightsবাংলা
  • ❦ 2011 · Written for the Kormojibi Nari anniversary,… Recollections of Kormojibi Nari Recalls Dr. Hossain's association with Shirin Akhtar and the workers' rights organisation Kormojibi Nari since it formed in 1991. It describes their joint work with ASK on lay-offs, garment workers' rights, the Yasmin and Shima custodial rape cases, factory disasters (Spectrum, KTS), minimum wage campaigns and organising agricultural workers. It ends by urging KN to stay with the workers and not be swayed by political loyalties. Tributes & Remembrances
  • ❦ 2010 · SAHR regional conference “Challenges to Peace… Welcome Address: SAHR Conference on Challenges to Peace and Prospects of Cooperation in South Asia Dr. Hossain's welcome speech for South Asians for Human Rights (SAHR). It expresses solidarity with Pakistan's flood victims and argues that South Asia is at a crossroads between economic promise and poverty, conflict, extremism and state impunity. It introduces SAHR's history since the Neemrana Declaration and its work on minorities and lawless law enforcement, and urges citizens to press SAARC governments to act. Speeches, Talks & Lectures
  • ❦ 2010 · South Asians for Human Rights Welcome Address: SAHR Meeting, Kathmandu Dr. Hossain's welcome address to an SAHR members' meeting, ten years after the Neemrana declaration. It surveys threats to peace and rights in South Asia: India-Pakistan hostility, militancy, drone strikes, abuses under anti-terror laws, discrimination against minorities, and displacement in Sri Lanka, Baluchistan and the Chittagong Hill Tracts. It outlines SAHR's studies, its parliament watch, and its call for citizens' peace dialogues. Speeches, Talks & Lectures
  • ❦ 2008 · South Asians for Human Rights A Human Rights Commission Must Have Effective Powers She responds to the Human Rights Commission Ordinance of 23 December 2007, welcoming it but criticising how it was drafted without consultation. She argues that the bureaucrat-dominated selection committee, the three-member size, the lack of independent powers of action and the exclusion of security forces will weaken it. She calls for amendments, public reports and real powers to deal with extrajudicial killings and custodial torture. Human Rights, Justice & Religion
  • ❦ 2008 · The Daily Star Women's Policy: What's the Fuss About? She defends the National Policy for Women's Development, which the Chief Adviser of the Caretaker Government announced on 8 March. She argues that it only restates earlier commitments (the Constitution, CEDAW, Beijing) and does not touch inheritance law. She criticises the protests by clerics and the four Advisers who apologised to them, and goes through the Policy section by section. She ends by calling for time-bound action plans and budgets. Gender & Development
  • ❦ 2007 · Forum Bangladesh 2006: A Human Rights Audit A year-end review of Bangladesh's human rights record in 2006. It covers RAB 'crossfire' killings, militant bombings, political violence, mass arrests, attacks on Hindu, Buddhist and Ahmadiya minorities, police shootings in Kansat and Phulbari, garment workers' wage protests and attacks on journalists. It concludes that political democracy had become 'captive' to a winner-takes-all culture and dysfunctional institutions. Human Rights, Justice & Religion
  • ❦ 2004 Implementing Women's Rights through CEDAW: Report on CEDAW Committee's Hearings on Bangladesh's Fifth Periodic Report A first-hand account of the CEDAW Committee's July 2004 New York hearing on Bangladesh's Fifth Periodic Report, which Dr. Hossain and Ayesha Khanam attended for the shadow report by ASK, Bangladesh Mahila Parishad and Steps. It criticises the government's late and defensive report and its refusal to lift reservations to Articles 2 and 16(1)(c), quoting the official replies. It sets out the Committee's recommendations and what activists can do next. Gender & Development
  • ❦ 2003 · Reprinted in Rights and Realities Justice Gone Awry Argues that the failure to try Pakistani war criminals and local collaborators for the 1971 genocide and mass rape encouraged later military interference in civilian life. It links this to the indemnity law for 'Operation Clean Heart' and the deaths in military custody. It widens to the US-led war on Iraq, arguing that peace and security do not come from military power. Human Rights, Justice & Religion
  • ❦ A South Asian women's conference on peace Herstory: Women's Initiatives for Peace and Coping with War A personal talk tracing South Asian women's peace and solidarity work, from Partition in 1947 (including her grandmother turning a house in Hyderabad, Sindh into a hospital, where she helped at age eleven) to 1971, the Birangonas, the People's Tribunal, the Tokyo Tribunal of 2000 and the ICC Gender Caucus. It contrasts women's caring and justice-seeking responses with indifferent or coercive state responses, and recalls cross-border solidarity, including Pakistani women's apologies for 1971. 1971, War & History

Tributes & Remembrances
  • ❦ On Ayesha Khanam A tribute to Ayesha Khanam of Bangladesh Mahila Parishad, tracing her path from 1960s student activist to women's-rights leader. It recalls her campaigns for a Uniform Family Code, against fatwa violence (the Nurjehan case), and for justice for Yasmin, a 14-year-old domestic worker raped and killed by police. It also covers her leadership in CEDAW shadow reporting and her openness to younger feminists. Tributes & Remembrances
  • ❦ Event at Bengal Foundation / Bengal Gallery… Remembering Professor Abdur Razzaq A talk given at the Bengal Foundation on the display of Professor Abdur Razzaq's book collection. Dr. Hossain recalls how 'Sir' introduced her to Bengal's commerce and culture, urged her to learn Persian for her textile research, and connected her with nationalist and artistic circles. She stresses his tolerance of dissent, his legal challenge on teachers' political rights and his warnings about two-thirds majorities, and ends with quotations from him on discrimination. Tributes & Remembrances
  • ❦ Review: The Role of Women in Preventing Violent Extremism A short review of an exploratory study by Dr Amena Mohsin, with Mahfuza Liza and Rajan Hossain, on women and violent extremism in Bangladesh, based on fieldwork in Cox's Bazar, Rajshahi and Ghazipur.   • ❦ Traditional Chakma Weaves A short descriptive note on back-strap loom (bain tant) weaving among Chittagong Hill Tracts communities, especially the Chakma. It recalls meeting Hasan at his wife's shop Rupayan, his political satire, his leadership of BSCIC design training for women, and his role as first president of the National Crafts Council with Dr. Hossain as Secretary, including the London exhibition.   • ❦ আনিসুজ্জামানের সাথে দেখা-সাক্ষাতের স্মৃতি Dr. Hossain remembers meeting Anisuzzaman in the 1960s at Professor Abdur Razzaq's gatherings. After she came to Dhaka in 1964, he was one of her teachers, with Munier Chowdhury and Mofazzal Haider Chowdhury, in the Bangla Academy course for non-Bengalis that she took with Salma Sobhan.
