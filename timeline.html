A life in its times

Hameeda Hossain's life alongside the movements and upheavals it moved through, from Partition to the garment workers' struggles of today. Choose what to follow, and jump between decades.

1930s1940s1950s1960s1970s1980s1990s2000s2010s2020s
  1. 1930s

  2. 28 December 1936Her lifeHyderabad, Sindh (British India)

    Born in Hyderabad, Sindh

    Hameeda Akhund was born in her grandmother's house in Hyderabad, Sindh, then part of British India, the youngest of six children of a district judge. She grew up between Sindhi family traditions and a convent, English-medium schooling.

  3. 1940s

  4. 1942–1943HistorySindh

    Hur uprising and martial law in Sindh

    The British crushed the Hur insurgency led by the Pir of Pagaro with special laws and martial law in Sindh from June 1942 to May 1943; the Pir was hanged in March 1943.

    Her father was a judge hearing Hur cases in Khairpur, and the unrest led her mother to move the children to Karachi; she recalls it in her memoir.

  5. c. 1944–1945Her lifeKarachi

    Schooldays in wartime Karachi

    She cycled to St Joseph's Convent in a mixed, cosmopolitan Karachi full of soldiers in the last years of the Second World War. The date is approximate: her memoir says the family moved in the mid-1940s when she was about ten.

  6. August 1947HistorySouth Asia

    Partition of British India

    India and Pakistan became independent on 14–15 August 1947 amid mass migration and communal violence across Punjab and Bengal.

    She saw Hindu classmates and neighbours leave Hyderabad and injured refugees arrive by train; she wrote about it in her memoir and her BDI lecture.

    Read: The Challenge of Citizenship in Building a Just Society (BDI Lifetime Achievement Award Lecture) →

  7. 1947Her lifeHyderabad, Sindh

    Caring for Partition refugees

    Aged about eleven, she was enlisted by her grandmother, Ghulam Fatima Shaikh, to help nurse refugees arriving in Hyderabad in an improvised hospital and shelter. She later called it one of two experiences that shaped her belief in a secular, humane response to violence.

    Read: The Challenge of Citizenship in Building a Just Society (BDI Lifetime Achievement Award Lecture) →
  8. 1950s

  9. c. 1949–1950Her lifeHyderabad, Sindh

    Matriculation from St Mary's Convent

    She passed her Matriculation from St Mary's Convent in Hyderabad, Sindh, after most Hindu students had left the city. She gives the year as 1949/1950, so it is approximate.

  10. 21 February 1952HistoryDhaka, East Pakistan

    The Language Movement

    Students in Dhaka were killed by police while demanding recognition of Bangla as a state language of Pakistan, a turning point for Bengali nationalism.

    She was in Sindh and New York at the time, but in Dhaka from 1963 she worked among scholars of the Language Movement, such as Kamruddin Ahmad, and wrote that it spurred the wider nationalist debate.

  11. early 1952Her lifeNew York, USA

    Herald Tribune Forum in New York

    An essay on 'the world we want', written as a first-year student at Hyderabad College, won her a place representing Pakistan at the New York Herald Tribune Forum for high-school students. She spent about three months with American host families, spoke at the Forum and met President Truman in Washington.

  12. 1954Her lifeWellesley, Massachusetts, USA

    Scholarship to Wellesley College

    With a recommendation from the Forum's director, Helen Hiett Waller, she won a scholarship to Wellesley College and entered as a sophomore. She studied English and history and took a writing workshop where she wrote about her grandmother.

  13. 1957Her lifeNew York / London

    Graduates; UN internship and London

    After graduating she interned in the UN Secretariat's information unit in New York, then spent six months in London working for a women's organisation that helped women's groups register to vote. Her CV gives the BA as 1958, but her interview says she was at Wellesley from 1954 to 1957 and her memoir has her home in Karachi by early 1958.

  14. October 1958HistoryPakistan

    Martial law in Pakistan

    President Iskander Mirza declared martial law in October 1958 and General Ayub Khan soon took power, banning political activity.

    Back in Karachi, she was warned by the authorities over her article 'Polygamy Outvoted' in Outlook, her first brush with censorship.

  15. 1958Her workKarachi

    Editor and writer in Karachi

    She returned to Karachi and began work as a researcher and editor at the Pakistan Institute of International Affairs, while helping edit the progressive weekly Outlook. She later joined Oxford University Press in Karachi as an editor.

  16. 1960s

  17. 1961HistoryPakistan

    Muslim Family Laws Ordinance

    Pakistan's Muslim Family Laws Ordinance regulated polygamy, divorce and marriage registration after campaigns by women's organisations.

    She traced her own awareness of the 'woman question' to reading women leaders' debates on the Ordinance, and later campaigned on its gaps.

  18. 1963Her lifeDhaka, East Pakistan

    First visit to Dhaka

    A month's visit to her sister in Dhaka drew her into university debates on autonomy, women's groups and artisan communities; she met Rehman Sobhan and Kamal Hossain and helped edit an issue of Sarwar Murshid's journal New Values. Most of her accounts say 1963, but two tributes give 1962 and a Forum draft says 1964.

    Read: The Challenge of Citizenship in Building a Just Society (BDI Lifetime Achievement Award Lecture) →

  19. 1964 or 1965Her lifeDhaka

    Marriage to Kamal Hossain

    She married the lawyer Dr Kamal Hossain and settled in Dhaka, where she set about learning Bangla. Wikipedia and her Anisuzzaman tribute point to 1964; her BDI lecture and career notes say she came to live in Dhaka in 1965.

  20. mid-1960sHer workDhaka

    An artisans' outlet in Dhanmondi

    With her sister and friends she opened a garage outlet in Dhanmondi so village artisans could sell directly to urban buyers rather than through middlemen. Her notes call it Srijani; her Karika history and BDI lecture call it Arunima.

  21. 1966HistoryEast Pakistan

    The Six Points movement

    The Awami League's Six Points programme for East Pakistan's autonomy drew mass support and state repression.

    She first encountered Dhaka through university debates on implementing the Six Points, which Forum and her friends' circle later argued for in print.

  22. late 1960sHer lifeDhaka

    Her daughters are born

    Her two daughters were born in Dhaka in the late 1960s; in March 1971 they were four and two. The elder, Sara Hossain, became a barrister and human-rights lawyer. The younger, Dina Hossain, became a documentary filmmaker, whose work began at PBS in the United States, and now teaches documentary filmmaking at BRAC University.

  23. January–March 1969HistoryDhaka

    The 1969 mass uprising

    A student-led mass uprising in East Pakistan forced the withdrawal of the Agartala case, the release of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman and the fall of Ayub Khan.

    She marched with students in 1969 and recalled 'shouting slogans for freedom' with Ayesha Khanam.

    Read: On Ayesha Khanam →

  24. November 1969Her workDhaka

    Forum's first issue

    She was editor, with Rehman Sobhan as executive editor and Kamal Hossain as publisher, of Forum, an English-language political weekly run from a garage room in Circuit House Road. Its first editorial was 'In Search of Freedom'. Permission had come in 1967 or 1968 (her accounts differ), and her CV dates her editorship 1967–71.

  25. 1970s

  26. April 1970MovementsDhaka

    Bangladesh Mahila Parishad founded

    Women's groups came together under Begum Sufia Kamal as the Mahila Parishad, pressing for family-law reform and women's representation.

    She sat in the audience at its inauguration at the Purbani Hotel and later worked with it on CEDAW shadow reports.

    Read: On Ayesha Khanam →

  27. 1–6 March 1971Her workDhaka

    Dhaka under siege

    After the National Assembly was postponed on 1 March, protests and curfews followed. She visited Dhaka Medical College Hospital and reported the dead and injured in Forum's 6 March issue.

  28. 7 March 1971HistoryDhaka

    Non-cooperation movement

    Sheikh Mujibur Rahman's 7 March speech launched a civil disobedience movement across East Pakistan.

    Her home became a hub where officials and party workers brought news, as Kamal Hossain helped draft the daily directives.

  29. 25 March 1971Her workDhaka

    Crackdown closes Forum

    The Pakistan army's attack on 25 March began the genocide in Bangladesh and ended Forum; the annual issue, with the editorial 'Options for a Sane Man', never reached the press.

  30. March–December 1971HistoryBangladesh

    The Liberation War

    Over nine months of war, the Pakistan army carried out mass killings and widespread sexual violence; Bangladesh won independence on 16 December 1971.

    Kamal Hossain was arrested and held in Haripur jail while she spent the war in Karachi with her daughters; the war's impact on women became central to her later work.

  31. January 1972Her lifeRawalpindi – London – Dhaka

    Flight home to Dhaka

    After the war she and her daughters were taken to Kamal Hossain's side on the plane that carried Sheikh Mujibur Rahman out of Pakistan, via London and Delhi. They landed to crowds in Dhaka, where Kamal Hossain was soon sworn in as a minister.

  32. 1972MovementsDhaka / Kushtia

    Working with women survivors of war

    She volunteered at the Women's Rehabilitation Centre set up by Begum Sufia Kamal and Badrunnesa Ahmed, and helped war-widowed women in a Kushtia village and in Mirpur form groups to stitch and sell kanthas. She kept one of those kanthas as a symbol of their survival.

    Her own direct work in the post-war women's movement.

  33. 4 November 1972HistoryDhaka

    Constitution of Bangladesh adopted

    The new Constitution enshrined nationalism, socialism, democracy and secularism; Kamal Hossain chaired its drafting committee.

    Karika later chose cooperative ownership to fit Article 13 on people's ownership of production, and she often cited the Constitution's equality clauses.

  34. 1972–1974Her workDhaka

    Editor at Oxford University Press, Dhaka

    Declining the role of a minister's wife, she went back to editing at Oxford University Press in Dhaka.

  35. November 1973Her workDhaka

    National crafts exhibition

    After touring from Rangamati to Rangpur and Sylhet to Rajshahi, she and friends brought village artisans to a large crafts exhibition in Dhaka that drew requests for a permanent outlet.

  36. November 1974Her workDhaka

    KARIKA cooperative registered

    KARIKA, a handicraft marketing cooperative owned and managed by artisans, was registered with the Cooperative Department; she was one of its nine founders. It welcomed women artisans, including Birangona survivors of 1971.

  37. June–July 1975MovementsMexico City

    UN World Conference on Women, Mexico City

    The first UN women's conference opened the UN Decade for Women (1976–85).

    She attended and spoke on a panel on crafts, meeting feminists and craft leaders such as Jasleen Dhamija and Devaki Jain.

    Read: The Challenge of Citizenship in Building a Just Society (BDI Lifetime Achievement Award Lecture) →

  38. 15 August 1975HistoryDhaka

    Assassination of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman

    Sheikh Mujibur Rahman and most of his family were killed in a military coup.

    She was in Dhaka with her daughters while Kamal Hossain was abroad; she and the children sheltered with friends before leaving for exile.

  39. September 1975Her lifeOxford, UK

    Exile in Oxford

    She and her daughters left for Oxford, where the family lived until about 1981–82, sharing life with other Bangladeshi friends in exile.

  40. c. 1976Her lifeOxford / London

    Doctoral research on Bengal's weavers

    Encouraged by Tapan Raychaudhuri, she enrolled at St Antony's College and studied East India Company records in the India Office Library on Bengal's textile trade. The start date is not given; she says the idea came after settling in Oxford in 1975.

  41. 1979Her work

    First scholarly article on weavers

    Her article 'Alienation of Weavers' on the East India Company's revenue and commercial interests appeared in the Indian Economic and Social History Review.

  42. 18 December 1979MovementsNew York

    CEDAW adopted by the UN

    The UN General Assembly adopted the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women.

    She spent decades holding Bangladesh to its CEDAW obligations through shadow and alternative reports.

    Read: Implementing Women's Rights through CEDAW: Report on CEDAW Committee's Hearings on Bangladesh's Fifth Periodic Report →
  43. 1980s

  44. 1982Her lifeOxford

    DPhil in Modern History, Oxford

    She completed her doctorate in modern history at St Antony's College, Oxford, and the family returned to Dhaka around 1981–82.

  45. 6 November 1984MovementsBangladesh

    Bangladesh ratifies CEDAW

    Bangladesh ratified CEDAW, with reservations to Articles 2 and 16.1(c).

    She joined a committee led by the Mahila Parishad that prepared shadow reports to the CEDAW Committee.

    Read: Implementing Women's Rights through CEDAW: Report on CEDAW Committee's Hearings on Bangladesh's Fifth Periodic Report →

  46. 1985Her workDhaka

    National Crafts Council of Bangladesh

    Inspired by the artist Qamrul Hasan, she helped found the National Crafts Council of Bangladesh and served as its secretary under his presidency. Her CV gives 1984 for its formation.

    Read: Tribute to Patua Qamrul Hasan →

  47. July 1985MovementsNairobi, Kenya

    UN World Conference on Women, Nairobi

    The conference closed the UN Decade for Women.

    She attended; discussions there on women's legal rights encouraged groups like hers to set up legal aid services, and she took part in DAWN's preparatory meeting.

    Read: The Challenge of Citizenship in Building a Just Society (BDI Lifetime Achievement Award Lecture) →

  48. 1986Her workDhaka

    Ain o Salish Kendra founded

    With Salma Sobhan, Khursheed Erfan Ahmed, Amirul Islam, F.H. Abed and others she founded Ain o Salish Kendra (ASK), starting with mediation and legal aid for women and workers from a borrowed garage in Dhanmondi. She was its Director of Research and Advocacy until 2003. Her lecture says 'early 1980s' and her timeline notes say 1985.

  49. 1987Her workDhaka

    Study of garment workers

    She co-authored a study of women garment workers with Roushan Jahan and Salma Sobhan, published in 1990 as No Better Options? Industrial Women Workers in Bangladesh. The same year saw her pieces on artisan design and craft markets.

  50. 1988Her workDelhi / Dhaka

    The Company Weavers of Bengal

    Oxford University Press, Delhi, published her doctoral study of how the East India Company controlled Bengal's weavers, 1750–1813. UPL later issued a Dhaka edition.

  51. 1990s

  52. December 1990HistoryDhaka

    Fall of Ershad

    A mass movement forced General H.M. Ershad's military-backed government to resign.

    She and fellow ASK founders took part in the movement against Ershad, and ASK documented abuses in its report 'Lawless Law Enforcement'.

  53. 26 March 1992MovementsDhaka

    The Gono Adalat

    Jahanara Imam's movement held a People's Tribunal in Dhaka on 1971 war crimes.

    ASK responded by recording oral histories of women survivors of 1971, which she helped edit.

  54. 1993MovementsMoulvibazar

    Nurjahan and the fatwa

    Nurjahan, a young woman in Moulvibazar, took her life after a village fatwa sentenced her to be stoned. The case set off a long campaign against fatwa violence.

    ASK and women's groups pursued the case. It led, about two decades later, to rulings against fatwa punishments, and she wrote about it repeatedly.

  55. August 1995MovementsDinajpur

    Yasmin's rape and murder

    Yasmin, a teenage domestic worker, was raped and killed by policemen in Dinajpur, sparking nationwide protests.

    ASK took up the case while she campaigned for recognition of domestic work; the policemen were convicted.

    Read: The Challenge of Citizenship in Building a Just Society (BDI Lifetime Achievement Award Lecture) →

  56. September 1995MovementsBeijing

    Beijing World Conference on Women

    The Fourth World Conference on Women adopted the Beijing Declaration and Platform for Action.

    She did not attend, but cited the Beijing Platform in her policy writing and recorded that Pakistani women at the NGO Forum apologised to Bangladeshi women for 1971.

  57. 1996Her workDhaka

    Editing ASK's human rights reports

    She began editing ASK's annual report, Human Rights in Bangladesh. Her résumé and bios say she edited it until 2006; her CV says 1996–2002.

  58. c. 1995–1997Her workNew York

    CEDAW shadow report taken to New York

    With Ayesha Khanam of the Mahila Parishad and others she drafted a shadow report and presented it to the CEDAW Committee in New York. Her tribute dates it 1995, but the Committee reviewed Bangladesh in 1997, so the exact year is uncertain.

    Read: On Ayesha Khanam →

  59. 1998Her workHerat, Afghanistan

    Witness to Taliban Afghanistan

    She went with Kamal Hossain, then UN Special Rapporteur for Afghanistan, on a mission by road from Mashhad to Herat, and wrote about women shut out of work and public life under the Taliban.

  60. 2000s

  61. July 2000MovementsNeemrana, India

    South Asians for Human Rights founded

    SAHR, a regional network of human-rights defenders, was founded at Neemrana, India, by Asma Jahangir, I.K. Gujral, Kamal Hossain and others.

    She later co-chaired SAHR and wrote about it in her tributes to Asma Jahangir.

    Read: In Memory of Asma Jahangir →

  62. 8–12 December 2000MovementsTokyo

    Women's Tribunal in Tokyo

    The Women's International War Crimes Tribunal on Japan's Military Sexual Slavery heard testimony from 'comfort women' survivors.

    She went to Tokyo with a Bangladeshi woman who was the first to speak publicly of the sexual violence she suffered in 1971.

  63. 1 January 2001MovementsDhaka

    High Court rules against fatwa punishments

    A High Court bench declared punishments imposed through fatwas illegal; the ruling was stayed on appeal.

    The litigation grew out of cases such as Nurjahan's that ASK had pursued.

  64. 2001Her workDhaka

    Narir Ekattor: women's voices of 1971

    ASK published oral histories of 22 women who lived through 1971, gathered by a team she worked with. The English translation, Rising from the Ashes, followed in 2013. Her timeline notes give 1997 for Narir Ekattor.

  65. 2004Her workDhaka

    CEDAW Shadow Report

    ASK, the Mahila Parishad and Steps Towards Development prepared a shadow report on the government's fifth periodic report, which she co-authored.

    Read: Implementing Women's Rights through CEDAW: Report on CEDAW Committee's Hearings on Bangladesh's Fifth Periodic Report →

  66. April 2005HistorySavar

    Spectrum factory collapse

    The Spectrum garment factory in Savar collapsed, killing dozens of workers.

    She recalled it in her writing on workers' safety and her tribute to Kormojibi Nari, years before Rana Plaza.

    Read: Recollections of Kormojibi Nari →

  67. 2008–2011Her workSouth Asia

    Co-Chairperson of SAHR

    She served as Co-Chairperson of South Asians for Human Rights, the regional human-rights network.

  68. May 2009MovementsDhaka

    Sexual harassment guidelines

    The High Court issued binding guidelines against sexual harassment in workplaces and educational institutions. She dates them 1 May 2009; press reports give 14 May.

    She cited the guidelines as an example of women's complaints leading to change.

    Read: The Challenge of Citizenship in Building a Just Society (BDI Lifetime Achievement Award Lecture) →
  69. 2010s

  70. 2010Her workNew Delhi / Dhaka

    Freedom from Fear, Freedom from Want

    She co-edited Freedom from Fear, Freedom from Want? Rethinking Security in Bangladesh, with her essay on women's experience of war. She also co-authored the 2010 Alternative Report to CEDAW.

  71. 12 May 2011MovementsDhaka

    Appellate Division bars fatwa punishments

    The Supreme Court's Appellate Division ruled that no punishment may be imposed through a fatwa.

    It closed the litigation that began with cases ASK took up, such as Nurjahan's in 1993.

  72. 24 November 2012HistoryAshulia, Dhaka

    Tazreen Fashions fire

    At least 112 workers died in a fire at the Tazreen garment factory in Ashulia.

    She campaigned for compensation and accountability for Tazreen's workers.

  73. 24 April 2013HistorySavar

    Rana Plaza collapse

    The Rana Plaza building in Savar collapsed, killing more than 1,100 garment workers.

    She wrote about it many times and led the campaign for compensation and safety.

  74. February–March 2014Her workDhaka

    Sramik Nirapotta Forum campaign

    As convenor of the Sramik Nirapotta Forum (Workers' Safety Forum), she led a two-month campaign before the first anniversary of Rana Plaza, demanding that brands and the state pay compensation.

    Read: It's Time to Pay Up →

  75. 2016Her workNew Delhi

    Of the Nation Born

    She co-edited Of the Nation Born: The Bangladesh Papers with Amena Mohsin (Zubaan), including her essay 'Bringing Women's Lives to Order'.

  76. 2020s

  77. April 2021Her work

    BDI Lifetime Achievement Award

    The Bangladesh Development Initiative named her its 2021 Lifetime Achievement awardee. In her November 2021 lecture, 'The Challenge of Citizenship in Building a Just Society', she traced how citizens worked for justice from 1947 onwards.

    Read: The Challenge of Citizenship in Building a Just Society (BDI Lifetime Achievement Award Lecture) →

  78. September 2023Her workDhaka

    Claiming Our Rights

    University Press Limited published Claiming Our Rights: Feminist Discourses in Bangladesh and South Asia, a collection of her essays on gender, violence, labour and human rights.
