Crossing the Boundaries

Describes how girls in Bangladesh have entered sport, which used to be a male domain, and mentions the national women's football team and the 2010 South Asian Games. The main example is Suborno Char in Noakhali, where Nijera Kori and the young feminist group One Billion Rising helped village girls aged 9 to 15 form a football team despite opposition from their conservative community.

By Hameeda HossainNovember 2018

Written for The Hunger Project (Nov 2018); place of publication not stated.

Uptil a few years ago sports were a mail domain in Bangladesh. In public stadiums, in college or school playing grounds boys teams competed with each other, winning trophies or being sent down by the referee. National teams for cricket and football were encouraged by the government. They were welcomed with flowers when they returned triumphantly from abroad. They were provided special training so that they could prepare for international and regional competitions.

Women or young girls were not to be seen in playing grounds. Most girls’ schools had no proper playing grounds. Where there was space, girls were restricted to tamer indoor games such as net ball. They were told that they did not have the physical energy to take on such strenuous activities, that girls should not be so mobile or visible in public spaces. Customs and traditions had prescribed their passive roles.

But that is an old story. Now the Bangladesh women's national football team is a national association affiliated to the Bangladesh Football Federation and a member of the Asian Football Confederation. Girls’ sports have been encouraged in educational institutions and with regular practice it has been possible to form a national team which has competed with teams from Bhutan, India, Nepal, Pakistan and Sri Lanka. Women's football was included in the 2010 South Asian Games hosted in Dhaka where the Bangladesh team won a bronze medal.

It is interesting to trace how young girls have crossed the boundaries of gender discrimination and exclusion in sports. They are no longer restricted within the four walls of their homes. Some schools and colleges have formed their own teams which compete on open fields. Even in villages girls are seen rolling their football in open spaces. And the national team has done us proud.

It hasn't been a simple matter of young girls showing an interest in sports. It has taken support from local organisations, as well as interaction with young feminists to encourage young girls to give up their inhibitions and for to bring about a change in conservative communities.

One such example we found in a small village in Noakhali. Suborno Char is probably one of the most conservative areas where girls could only go out at night so that they were not seen by anyone outside their family. When Nijera Kori suggested to local school girls that they learn to play football there was much hesitation at first. What would the elders say, would male gangs harass them, would Imams speak out against them?

They faced criticism from neighbours and worry from parents. But they overcame this. At first they played in the school yard. Gradually as they learnt the game they became enthusiastic to play in open spaces. Nijera Kori set up a camp where they invited young girls of 9 to 15 years to form a football team. When Nijera Kori brought a trainer from Dhaka the young girls were even more excited because she was a professional player and told them how she had played in different countries as well as in a stadium in Dhaka. As their playing skills improved so did their confidence in moving out of their cloistered existence, braving comments and stares. Since Nijera Kori had an office in the area its support particularly at the initial stage was very helpful in challenging village critics. To make the girls feel even more comfortable Nijera Kori invited a group of young feminists who had formed themselves into a group called One Billion Rising – indicating women who were freeing themselves from social constraints and making brave choices of work, life and friendships.

Members of One Billion Rising were young and enthusiastic and soon they organized a championship match at Char Jabbar, where they invited FIFA Council Member Mahfuza Akhter Kiron. The football players played even better knowing that her recognition might enable them to play in national games as well. No doubt the audience must have shared in the thrilling display of skilled players who shot the ball with abandon.

Suborno Char is only one example where young women have defied social prejudice and asserted their right to freedom. No doubt there are other places and other organisations which too are enabling young women to play without constraints. It is not only that they have learnt a new game but that they can be counted upon to resist discrimination.

And the girls of Suborno Char have done so not only for themselves but they have set an example for others to follow.
