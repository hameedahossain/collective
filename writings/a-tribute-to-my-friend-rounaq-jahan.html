A Tribute to My Friend Rounaq Jahan

Recalls Dr. Hossain's nearly fifty-year friendship with political scientist Rounaq Jahan, from their meeting in 1970 at Professor Razzaq's gatherings. It covers Jahan's founding role in Women for Women (1973), her participation in the 1975 Mexico UN Women's Conference, and her work in regional feminist networks. It ends by endorsing Bonik Barta's award to her.

By Hameeda HossainOctober 2022

Written for the honouring of Rounaq Jahan by Bonik Barta and BIDS. Read the original ↗

I have known Rounaq Jahan for almost five decades. We first met in 1970 when she had just returned from Harvard, after having submitted her PhD thesis. The political environment in Dhaka was very volatile, with demands for autonomy gaining ground. At Dhaka University academics and students were arguing for political changes.

Rounaq Jahan had recently published her thesis entitled Pakistan: Failure in National Integration, which provided rational and well researched arguments for where the then East Pakistan was heading. She was appointed as an Associate professor in the Department of Political Science at Dhaka University. In this position she was able to impart her learning and share her ideas with her students and colleagues. I think they found her very open and felt encouraged by her class lectures or in discussions outside the class room.

She became quite well known in intellectual circles as well. A small group of academics and professionals used to gather together in Professor Razzaq’s home just across from the Shahid Minar. Not only senior teachers such as Professor Anisuzaman and Professor Salahuddin joined in, but there were younger economists who were eager to join the political debates. Rounaq became a valuable member of this group as her arguments always drew upon non controversial evidence. We used to meet here and I remember being very impressed by her serious and reasoned contributions to the debates.

Alongside the national movement, the demand for women’s rights was also gaining ground in what was to become Bangladesh. The Bangladesh Mahila Parishad was officially inaugurated in early 1970. They were joined by students and professional women. Other smaller groups were forming themselves as protagonists of women’s political and personal rights. Rounaq Jahan was able to promote her ideas of women’s economic independence amongst these groups. She did this by organizing seminars which were well attended. In 1973 she took an initiative to start a women’s research organization which called itself Women for Women. Rounaq played a seminal role in its formation and was able to direct its research towards issues concerning women’s rights, of gender equality and justice. The organisation’s first volume was published under her leadership in 1975.

She became well known through her participation in these meetings and was invited to international feminist meetings. In 1975 she was invited to the UN Women’s Conference in Mexico where she spoke of policies for women’s development in Bangladesh. Her participation encouraged an international exchange of scholars. She helped to catalyse Bangladeshi women’s involvement in international and regional meetings. Her active role in promoting women’s rights contributed to her appointments to the APDC in Bangkok and later the ILO in Geneva.

Rounaq and I have developed a long standing relationship over the years, as we became involved in human rights and women’s rights meetings, both in Bangladesh and abroad. She encouraged me to attend the Mexico Conference where I had been asked to talk about women’s roles in craft production. This led to friendships with other feminists in the region, such as Devaki Jain and Vina Majumdar of India and Kumari Jayawardene of Sri Lanka. As a close group of feminists we were able to interact with other feminists in the South. At meetings organized by DAWN or APWLD or other regional networks we were able to identify with a South perspective on development. Rounaq Jahan has contributed to this both through her official work and through her writings.

Rounaq Jahan has played a seminal role in guiding the women’s struggle for change through her academic work and her political and women’s rights related activism. I feel that Bonik Barta’s decision to honour her with an award for her work is well deserved.
